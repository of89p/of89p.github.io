# A day with UNHCR <br> <sub style="font-size: 20px;">2 Jul 2026</sub>

In the glitzy Tun Razak Exchange mall downtown KL, I was fascinated by how nice it was. There were no plaster walls characteristic of KL malls. Everything here was properly done. Today, there was an event in the theatre. A special screening hosted by UNHCR and sponsored by the Canadian embassy. A blue board emblazoned with “Home for Hope” was prominently displayed. UN officials were running around hosting donors. With a corporate smile, I was told to leave the venue because the event hadn't yet begun: “Come back at 5”. 

When I returned, what greeted me were four booths selling delightful handcrafted products. Two of them sold crochet toys; There were green frogs with scrawny string arms and legs, an incredibly close-to-life Winnie the Pooh, countless miffy lookalikes and turtles of blue, violet and red; Most had the option to be converted to keychains. The center booth sold batik crafts. Handmade by artisans from the Rohingya Women Development Network, they featured all sorts of products. I marvelled at how jewellery holders, phone/water bottle holders, and tote bags were all made from fabric. These were beautiful fabrics with intricate designs. They had blue and yellow flowers, one was even an intense shade of turquoise. 

On the “Home for Hope” blue exhibition panel. What appeared to be careless scribbles turned out to be words of well-wishes from guests. There were phrases like “Stand with refugees” and “We welcome you!” written in shiny gold. The donors scrambled to take pictures in front of the board with wide beaming smiles. After which, they were led by UN officials to have some Champagne. Perhaps the UN forgot Malaysia is a Muslim country. No; “It is provided by them,” an employee told me, pointing to the white officials from the West. They all had blue stickers on their chest: “Hi I am _____”. Champagne glass in hand, they stood in circles and laughed loudly. They were then led to a buffet reception. 

My interpreter, a Rohingya refugee, told me they were “stupid”. The guy clearly did not like them. He told me he has been waiting for 16 years to be relocated to a better country, yet here he still was, forced to watch these officials with their white bosses sipping on Champagne in downtown KL. The refugees were faithfully manning their booths. I asked why not go for dinner. They were not invited, they told me. 

Slowly, we noticed the crowds thinning. They departed the booth area and trooped into the cinema. My interpreter smiled cheekily and asked the UN staff if he could go in. The staff shuffled through some papers and, looked up and said it was “for donors only”. I was puzzled. Is this not an event to celebrate the tenacity of refugees? “Its not like that,” the staff said quickly. 

At 6.30pm, the refugees were told to pack up. As they left the theatre, the atrium once again was empty. The donors and officials were still inside enjoying their show.
