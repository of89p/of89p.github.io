# Pico Iyer's "This Could be Home" <br> <sub style="font-size: 20px;">11 Jul 2026</sub>

Perhaps the most striking feature that can summarise what this book is, is how it ends. A book centred around the Raffles Hotel, it ends with Raffles Institutions' motto. This book is everything Raffles and the most strongly the elitist charge that comes with it. Iyer only talks to people in power in this book: The General Manager of Raffles Hotel, high ranking public servants like the Director of the National Heritage Board, dinner with graduates from “the London School of Economics and MIT”, and Raffles’ “Resident Historian” (yes the hotel has a “historian”). 

I don’t know how to feel about this book. On the one hand, it is an ad; Sponsored by Raffles Hotel (Iyer is their very first “Writer-In-Recidence”) and the British Council. This book plays off like a satire of inauthentic travel writing: Instant access to corporate and civil service leaders and dining only at fine places. Iyer only ever hangs out at the downtown Orchard, Marina Bay and Beach Road area, never venturing into the Singapore heartlands. He claimed that “the fact remains that you can’t really say you’ve been to Singapore until you’ve stepped through the columned corridors of Raffles”. Urm, well, I haven’t. Perhaps I should return my Pink IC and find another nation to take me in. 

Granted, Iyer has a hard task balancing the lived Singapore experience and the high-class image Raffles commissioned him to present. But that is precisely the problem because good travel writing has to be done with no preconceived experience, let alone a financially sponsored one. 

Yet, this was still an enjoyable read. Iyer’s description of after-hours Orchard, the Apple Store in Orchard and his MRT ride framed his experience here in a strange new angle such that I tell myself, wow, I have been here before. That is so strange because good reading hardly features Singapore as setting, let alone places where I spend weekends with friends sometimes. 

I wish Iyer would spend more time perhaps at Bishan or Chua Chu Kang. It is hard to claim this is a travel writing piece because, unless Singapore were only a hundred square kilometres of downtown area, Iyer never truly visited Singapore. It is hard to believe “This could be home”. He hasn’t even visited it yet.
